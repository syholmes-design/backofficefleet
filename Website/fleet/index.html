Trip readiness doctrine

BOF reviews the load at the start, middle, and end of the trip.

Readiness is not just document storage. BOF keeps the release gate, active-trip watch items, closing packet, owner, and consequence visible so dispatch, safety, billing, and settlements are not guessing from disconnected uploads.

Release control standard

No load releases until the driver, carrier, equipment, cargo, route, facility, and commercial packet are ready. No load closes until delivery proof, asset return, claim review, settlement, billing, and archive are complete.

Start

Start readiness review

Before release, BOF checks commercial terms, driver, carrier, equipment, cargo, facility, route, accessorials, and the final release decision.

Middle

Middle readiness review

While the load is active, BOF watches pickup proof, securement, route/HOS risk, equipment exceptions, accessorial capture, and claim signals.

End

End readiness review

Before close, BOF checks POD quality, cargo outcome, asset return, defect status, billing support, settlement readiness, archive, and next-load availability.

DVIR handling

BOF uses last DVIR review and open defect clearance in the start packet. A no-defect confirmation may be recorded by company policy, but BOF does not present a formal DVIR as a universal requirement for every property-carrying load.

Load packet readiness

The release packet carries commercial terms, customer instructions, BOL expectations, accessorial rules, and required proof. That packet is separate from the equipment condition check.

While the load is active, BOF keeps watch items attached to the trip.

End readiness review

The load does not close just because it delivered.

BOF treats delivery as the start of the closeout review, not the end of the work. The trip closes only after proof, claim, settlement, billing, asset, and archive records support the final answer.

Post-trip defect review

BOF captures post-trip DVIR if a defect exists, plus maintenance notes and equipment photos. If no defect is reported, BOF can record that company-policy confirmation without turning it into a universal DVIR claim.

Billing/factoring readiness

The invoice, rate confirmation, signed POD, lumper or detention proof, settlement notes, and claim clearance stay together so billing does not outrun the operating record.
