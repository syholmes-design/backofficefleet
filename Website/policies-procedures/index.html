Required policy workspace

The policy library works like an operating control system, not a document shelf.

Each row keeps the title, category, version, effective date, owner, acknowledgment state, review status, and a working route or section action. Long policy summaries stay out of the way until a reviewer needs them.

The goal is not to publish a wall of legal language. The page shows how BOF can make a written operating rule usable by dispatch, safety, billing, driver-file, and business-office owners at the moment a record needs review.

Governance workflow

A written rule becomes useful only after assignment, acknowledgment, and audit evidence are visible.

  1. 1Drafted

    Owner creates or updates rule.

  2. 2Reviewed

    Functional lead checks operating fit.

  3. 3Approved

    Decision owner records approval.

  4. 4Published

    Current version replaces old copy.

  5. 5Assigned

    Workers or roles receive the rule.

  6. 6Acknowledged

    Completion state is tracked.

  7. 7Audited

    Evidence and exceptions are reviewed.

  8. 8Revised

    Updates return to the library.

Proof before explanation

Policy control is shown through metadata, not long summaries.

BOF can keep acknowledgment, version history, assigned responsibility, exception handling, and audit evidence beside the operating workflow that depends on the rule.

That matters because the person using the rule is often under time pressure. The driver-file owner, dispatcher, safety lead, or billing reviewer needs the current rule, the owner, and the action path before they need the full written procedure.

Operational consequence

Procedure gaps should produce assigned work, not vague risk.

When a policy is due, blocked, or missing acknowledgment, the next owner is clear. Driver readiness, dispatch release, safety review, settlement billing, and business operations can all show the rule that controls the decision.

This keeps policy management close to operating work. A driver-file policy can affect readiness, a billing-packet rule can affect invoice preparation, and an access-governance rule can affect who may update sensitive records. The library gives each consequence a path back to the page where the work is happening.
