Trust controls

Governance is practical: role, owner, history, retention cue, exception, approval, and evidence.

Role-based accessPeople see the records their work requires.

Examples show access by role and owner without claiming a specific certification or technical standard.

Record ownershipEvery operational record needs an accountable owner.

Driver documents, dispatch releases, safety exceptions, and finance records can each identify who must act next.

Change historyChanges carry time, reason, and affected record.

The audit table shows what changed and why, so the next reviewer does not rely on memory.

Document retentionRetention cues stay visible for owner review.

BOF can organize retention references while fleets keep professional review where required.

Exception assignmentHeld and blocked records show owner follow-up.

Exceptions are useful when the status, owner, due date, and consequence are visible.

Evidence traceabilityProof links back to the operating surface.

Evidence actions route to driver profiles, operations records, safety, settlements, or policies.

Approval recordsApprovals explain the decision boundary.

A release, hold, correction, or policy update should show whether approval is complete, current, in progress, or still under review.

Data governance

Trust pages should support the operating story without overpromising security or compliance outcomes.

The public page should show control discipline: who owns data, what record changed, which decision was made, and where proof lives. Sensitive technical controls, legal advice, and customer-specific obligations belong in controlled review, not in unsupported public claims.

That keeps the page credible. It gives buyers a way to inspect accountability while leaving sensitive technical detail, contracts, and customer-specific control requirements for the proper review setting.
