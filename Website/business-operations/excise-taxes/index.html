Excise Taxes & Federal Fleet Compliance

For carriers operating commercial motor vehicles, excise tax compliance is operational. BOF manages the filing calendar, source documents, owner follow-up, and retention trail so filings are timely, records are audit-ready, and trucks stay road-legal.

The Excise Tax Landscape for Motor Carriers

Commercial trucking carries a heavier compliance burden than ordinary businesses because equipment weight, jurisdiction mileage, fuel consumption, and carrier authority all create separate administrative obligations. The work touches IRS Form 2290, IFTA fuel tax reports, IRP apportioned registration, UCR registration, and state weight-distance programs.

BOF does not replace a CPA, tax preparer, or agency filing authority. Its role is to keep the operating record complete: obligation, due date, owner, source document, receipt, exception, and clearance path.

Clearance Workflow

  1. Capture the obligation. BOF records tax type, unit, jurisdiction, due date, owner, support documents, and filing status.
  2. Attach source evidence. Fuel receipts, mileage schedules, cab cards, stamped returns, and confirmation numbers stay with the record.
  3. Classify status. Each obligation is ready, due, under review, blocked by document, or blocked by owner action.
  4. Escalate consequence. The record explains whether the issue affects a unit, lane, registration renewal, payment, or audit trail.
  5. Retain proof. Final filings and receipts stay available for review, renewal, audit, or leadership reporting.
