# 50-state + DC MVR access matrix (2026-10-17)

**How verified.** The research ran six parallel web searches. The proxy blocked direct fetches of state sites, so every claim comes from **search-result excerpts**.
- **[O] = Official:** the excerpt came from an official state page, form, rule or statute. The wording is official but may be out of date.
- **[S] = Secondary:** vendor, third-party or older material.
- **[U] = Unverified.**

**No row has been confirmed with the agency.** "Verified eligibility" below means an official text says so. Everything else is an assumption until the state confirms in writing.

Ohio, Michigan, Georgia and Missouri come from [`state-mvr-plan.md`](state-mvr-plan.md) and were not re-researched.

**Columns**
- **Direct:** a business can obtain MVRs from the state (account, contract or subscription).
- **Electronic:** what exists beyond mail.
- **Notification:** a state-run employer notification or monitoring service.
- **Agent for many carriers** (can BOF itself enrol and serve several carriers?):
  - **Yes [O]:** official text allows a provider, agent or reseller.
  - **Likely:** a subscriber or redisclosure model that does not exclude it.
  - **Unclear:** nothing found either way.
  - **Restricted:** official text limits it.
- **Portal:** the state's e-government portal operator. "Tyler" means Tyler Technologies (formerly NIC). It is one vendor, but **each state is a separate subscriber agreement**.

Every state requires its **own agreement**. **No recognized multi-state arrangement grants MVR access in more than one state** (see `interstate-access-report.md`).

## What the matrix says
- **Direct business access:** some form exists in essentially every state. Exceptions: CT (mail only), NH/NV/SD/WY (paper, often notarized) and OK (bulk restricted).
- **Official text supports a provider or agent serving multiple employers:** CA, FL, GA, MN, MO, NJ, TX. "Leans yes": WA, MI. **Likely** in about 12 more. **Restricted:** PA, OK, HI, NV, NH, DC.
- **Electronic batch or API:** AL (web service), AZ, GA (web service), KS, LA, MD (REST API), MN (daily change file), NE, NJ, WA (data contract), TX.
- **State notification or monitoring services (~20):** AL, AR, CA, DC, HI, ID, IN, ME, MD, MA, MI, MN (feed), NE, NY, OR, SC, TX (unconfirmed), UT, VA, WA, WV, WI. **Third-party enrolment for multiple employers is not addressed in any official text found,** except California's EPN agents.
- **CDL verification:** in every state the CDL MVR is the authoritative record of status, class, endorsements and (since 2025) medical certification. AAMVA's **CDLIS employer check** is the only national lookup. It tells you where a driver is licensed and their prior licenses, **not** their driving history (see the interstate report).
- **Tyler Technologies** runs the business portal in about 20 of these states. That is one vendor relationship to understand, but still **one subscriber agreement per state**.

### State MVR direct-access research, group 1 (AL, AK, AZ, AR, CA, CO, CT, DE, DC, FL, HI, ID)

Researched 2026-10-17. **Method limits:** direct page fetches failed for most hosts (azdot.gov, dmv.alaska.gov, alabamainteractive.atlassian.net, and embarksafety.com were all DNS/proxy-blocked). Every claim below therefore comes from **search-result snippets**. Where the snippet came from an official state domain, the claim is marked [Official-snippet]. The shared web-search budget ran out before follow-up checks could be done for AL subscription fees, the FMCSA ENS state chart, CT, DC fees, and the reseller clauses in several states. Those claims are marked [Unverified] and are listed as open items at the end.

**Applies to every state:**
- CDL verification: under 49 CFR 383.73, each state must post the CDL holder's medical-certification status on the CDLIS driver record. A CDL-type MVR therefore normally shows license class, endorsements, restrictions, status, and medical-certificate status. [Official-federal rule, from background knowledge; not re-fetched]
- Every state requires its own agreement or account. No multi-state DMV agreement turned up. The only cross-state commonality is the portal operator: Tyler Technologies (formerly NIC) runs the AL, AR, CO, HI, and ID portals.
- DPPA (18 USC 2721(b)(2)/(b)(3)/(b)(9) for employer and CDL purposes) and driver consent are needed everywhere.

#### Per-state notes and sources

**Alabama.** High-volume access runs through Alabama Interactive's ALEA DHR Web Service (XML/JSON). Prerequisites are an active AI customer account with an annual subscription, ALEA approval, and an ALEA-assigned DPPA number per user. The Driver Monitoring Service watches DHRs for citations, crashes, administrative actions, and status changes. It returns "NOCHANGESFOUND" at no charge and pulls the DHR at the normal fee when something changed. Vendors list ~$10 (Verisk: rose from $9.75 to $10 in 2018). There is no official word on whether a service provider may hold one account for many carriers.
Sources: https://alabamainteractive.atlassian.net/wiki/x/VoI9 ; https://alabamainteractive.atlassian.net/wiki/x/AoDwz ; https://alea.gov/sites/default/files/dl-forms/DLDriverRecordRequest.pdf ; https://www.verisk.com/blog/average-fees-for-motor-vehicle-reports-continue-to-rise/

**Alaska.** $10 per record, delivered as PDF. Employers send Form 419, and companies can use the "Company Release for Multiple Driving Records", to doa.dmv.research@alaska.gov and are invoiced electronically. CDL drivers must select the "CDL Employment" record. No bulk/API or notification service was found. A third-party agent would need each driver's consent naming it, which is unconfirmed.
Sources: https://dmv.alaska.gov/credential-services/driving-record/ ; https://online.dmv.alaska.gov/onlinedrivingrecords

**Arizona.** Electronic Data Services gives "authorized government agencies and commercial companies" interactive or batch access to DL MVRs. It requires an ADOT agreement and MVRRS access. (The separate Authorized Third Party program covers in-person MVD transactions and is not relevant.) Embark 2025 lists fees of $6 (3-yr) and $8 (5-yr).
Sources: https://azdot.gov/motor-vehicles/professional-services/commercial-services/electronic-data-services ; https://apps.azdot.gov/files/mvd/mvd-forms-lib/96-0484.pdf

**Arkansas.** DFA charges $13 online and $10 mail for the commercial record. The driver release is valid 5 years. Driver Watch (via Tyler Arkansas, $150/yr subscription) monitors Arkansas CDL validity, can email status changes, and auto-pulls a full record ($13). FMCSA's 2018 chart lists Driver Watch as an ENS, at $95/yr. The fee pages may be dated.
Sources: https://www.arkansas.gov/driverwatch/aboutdw.html ; https://dfa.arkansas.gov/driver-services/driver-records ; https://dfa.arkansas.gov/wp-content/uploads/Requestor_Form-2022.pdf ; https://www.fmcsa.dot.gov/sites/fmcsa.dot.gov/files/docs/registration/commercial-drivers-license/405406/jurisdictional-ens-implementation-final_0.pdf

**California.** This is the clearest path for a service provider. The Commercial Requester Account has a **Service Provider variant (INF 1106V / instructions INF 1133V)**; INF 1106 is for "end users only". The fee is $50, or $250 plus a $50,000 bond (INF 1132) for residence-address access. Accounts renew every 2 years. Since 4/1/2026 all applications, invoices, and requests are electronic. EPN is mandatory for CDL employers. DMV maintains a list of pre-approved EPN agents, and an employer authorizes one with INF 2110, which names both the employer's and the agent's requester codes. Each carrier still needs its own EPN requester code. The default rule is no resale or retention except as DMV authorizes.
Sources: https://dmv.ca.gov/portal/file/commercial-requester-account-application-service-provider-inf-1106v-pdf ; https://www.dmv.ca.gov/portal/?p=1792 ; https://dmv.ca.gov/portal/file/employer-pull-notice-program-requirements-inf-1107-pdf ; https://www.dmv.ca.gov/portal/vehicle-industry-services/motor-carrier-services-mcs/employer-pull-notice-epn-program/employer-pull-notice-epn-program-faqs/ ; https://dmv.ca.gov/portal/uploads/2022/09/INF-2110-R10-2015-AS-WWW.pdf

**Colorado.** Colorado Interactive runs "Driver History Interactive" (forms go to CI, which forwards them to DOR; free for government). The DR 2489 Affidavit of Intended Use is mandatory. Fees are $9.25 non-certified and $10.25 certified (rev 6/2025). The commercial subscription fee was not found. Colorado was an FMCSA ENS pilot but has no confirmed state ENS.
Sources: https://dmv.colorado.gov/sites/dmv/files/documents/DR2489.pdf ; https://www.cde.state.co.us/transportation/coloradointeractive ; https://colorado.public.law/statutes/crs_42-1-206

**Connecticut.** Officially the fee is $20, and another person's record can be requested by mail only. No current business or bulk program was found. 2014 press described a proposed business portal and said bulk sales went to insurers only. This row is the **least certain**; contact the CT DMV Copy Records unit.
Sources: https://portal.ct.gov/dmv/licenses-permits-ids/request-driving-record ; https://ctsenaterepublicans.com/?p=20814

**Delaware.** The Direct Access Program requires an application and a signed contract, bills monthly, and charges $25 per record (J.J. Keller). The portal is extranet.dmv.de.gov, and a 2021 notice describes automated applications and renewals. Embark says DE records show convictions only.
Sources: https://extranet.dmv.de.gov/search.ejs ; https://jjkellercompliancenetwork.com/regsense/driving-records-delaware ; https://dmv.de.gov/forms/veh_serv_forms/pdfs/personal_rel_form.pdf

**District of Columbia.** The Driver Record Online Portal is for employers and regulators to monitor employee records (3/5/10-yr/full). Enrollment needs the company's letterhead, business license, and charter or annual report or financials, plus written employee authorization. It is structured for the employer itself; whether an agent can enroll for many employers is unconfirmed. Fees were not found.
Sources: https://dmv.dc.gov/page/driver-record-online-portal

**Florida.** Access is through the FLHSMV Data Exchange MOU. It explicitly contemplates "Third Party End Users", with the requesting party responsible for their compliance and written contracts required for redisclosure, so it is reseller-friendly. Fees are $8 (3-yr) and $10 (7-yr/complete) (HSMV 90511). There is a separate electronic DL status report at $0.50/item (FS 320.05). No state ENS was found; vendors run Florida "weekly status monitoring" products.
Sources: https://www.flhsmv.gov/pdf/forms/90511.pdf ; https://agenda.oneclay.net/content/files/flhmv-data-exchange-mou-1223.pdf ; https://florida.public.law/statutes/fla._stat._320.05 ; https://m.flsenate.gov/Statutes/322.20

**Hawaii.** Records are Judiciary traffic abstracts (court convictions), not DMV license records; license and CDL status would come from county DMVs (unverified). The eHawaii Kalaiwa bulk service requires a letterhead request signed by an officer, a certificate of good standing, and a subscription agreement ($75). Abstracts are $23 each. Driver-history-lookup monitoring is $0.15/driver/month and auto-orders an abstract when a new judgment appears. Data is "single use" and "may not be resold multiple times".
Sources: https://portal.ehawaii.gov/home/online-services/bulk-traffic-abstracts/ ; https://portal.ehawaii.gov/page/help/subscriber-account/purchase-bulk-traffic-abstracts/ ; https://portal.ehawaii.gov/docs/BecominganAbstractCustomerForm.pdf

**Idaho.** The Tyler Idaho premium subscription is $95/yr for up to 100 users, with DL record searches at $10 each. The agreement bars resale or transfer for purposes prohibited by NMSA 66-2-7.1 or the DPPA, which implies resale for permitted purposes is possible. This is not confirmed. Sources: https://www.mvd.newmexico.gov/wp-content/uploads/2020/12/nmi-subscriber-services-checklist-business.pdf ; https://mvd.newmexico.gov/wp-content/uploads/2022/01/nmi-agreement-for-access.pdf ; https://www.mvd.newmexico.gov/dealers-auto-recyclers/access-to-nm-driver-and-vehicle-data/ .

**New York.** Dial-In (MV-15D) costs a $7 search fee, with escrow of at least $200 or two months of searches. It requires a DPPA certification and business records that exist before each search. LENS sends email on business days for convictions, crashes, suspensions, expirations, HazMat and medical-certification changes. Enrollment is per organization (FEIN, intended use, driver estimate) and takes about 2 weeks. LENS is separate from the 19-A monitoring service. No official text was found on whether a service provider can hold one LENS or Dial-In account for multiple employers. Vendors (e.g., CIChecked "Vigilant NYS DMV LENS") market LENS-based monitoring to clients [Secondary], which suggests some intermediary model exists. Ask Data Services. Sources: https://dmv.ny.gov/records/automated-access-for-business/dial-in ; https://dmv.ny.gov/records/escrow-accounts ; https://dmv.ny.gov/dmv-records/license-event-notification-service-lens ; https://dmv.ny.gov/records/automated-access-for-business/all-data-services-office-products ; https://www.cichecked.com/vigilant-nys-dmv-lens .

**North Carolina.** Release is governed by the DPPA and G.S. 20-43.1. Fees come from G.S. 20-26(c) and 20-42(b). The DPPA-1 form (rev. 2020) lists $10.75 uncertified and $15 certified. Online ordering through PayIt is for individuals with an NC DL. An NCDOT public-records response mentions "commercial use agreements" and bulk data at $0.03 per record (FY20–22). No business MVR program page was found. Sources: https://www.ncdot.gov/dmv/downloads/Documents/DL-DPPA-1.pdf ; https://dr-www.ncdot.gov/dmv/offices-services/online/Pages/driving-record-help.aspx ; https://www.muckrock.com/foi/north-carolina-153/commercial-licensing-list-141640/ .

**North Dakota.** Form SFN 51386 sets $3 per record, and the online portal sells a limited copy. Statute requires the full CDL record to go to an employer or prospective employer with the driver's written consent, for $3. 2025 bill 25-1344 drafts proposed $5–$6. Enactment is unverified. No business account program was found (search budget ran out). Sources: https://www.dot.nd.gov/forms/sfn51386.pdf ; https://ndlegis.gov/assembly/69-2025/regular/documents/25-1344-02000.pdf .

**Oklahoma.** Service Oklahoma charges $25, or $28 certified. Requests are by mail or in person, and online is for your own MVR only. OAC 260:135-1-5 limits bulk reports to courts, government, insurers, and others "as authorized by the Director or designee." An employer/agent volume arrangement would need Director approval. Sources: https://oklahoma.gov/content/dam/service-oklahoma/Documents/MVRequestforRecords-303RM0097.pdf ; https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/oklahoma/OAC-260-135-1-5 ; https://pay.apps.ok.gov/dps/mvr/app/ .

**Oregon.** The Record Inquiry Account costs $70, is open to entities qualified under ORS 802.175–.191, and is billed monthly. The Automated Reporting Service, for account holders' employees, mails a certified court print on new events. The employment driving record is 3 years and limited to on-the-job convictions and crashes. ORS 802.181 limits resale to DMV-authorized persons, requires a 5-year log, and bars resale to anyone "in the business of disseminating." OAR 735-010-0240 governs reseller authorization. *Oregon Trucking Ass'ns v. ODOT* (2019) held that DMV need not give resellers electronic access. Sources: https://www.oregon.gov/odot/DMV/pages/records/recinquiry_daveaccts.aspx ; https://www.oregon.gov/ODOT/DMV/Pages/records/available.aspx ; https://www.oregonlaws.org/ors/802.181 ; https://oregon.public.law/rules/oar_735-010-0240 .

Employers of CDL drivers can get "unlimited electronic access." The PennDOT release form bars transfer to anyone except the End User.
