1. Purpose and Scope

This Information Classification Policy establishes the comprehensive framework for classifying BOF's information assets based on sensitivity, business impact, and regulatory requirements. This policy applies to all information created, stored, transmitted, or processed by BOF, regardless of format or location.

Scope: All electronic and physical information assets, including but not limited to business documents, customer data, financial records, employee information, intellectual property, and system data.

2. Classification Levels
  • Public Information intended for general public distribution, no restrictions
  • Internal Company internal use only, limited to employees and contractors
  • Confidential Sensitive business information, restricted to authorized personnel
  • Restricted Highly sensitive information, need-to-know basis only
3. Classification Criteria

Information classification is based on multiple factors including business impact, legal requirements, sensitivity, and distribution needs.

  • Business Impact Assess potential harm to business operations, reputation, and financial position if compromised
  • Legal Requirements Consider regulatory obligations, contractual commitments, and legal protections
  • Sensitivity Evaluate information sensitivity, privacy implications, and competitive advantage
  • Distribution Needs Determine who needs access for business functions and operational requirements
4. Detailed Classification Framework
Classification Level Description Handling Requirements Examples
Public Information intended for general public distribution with no restrictions No special handling required Marketing materials, press releases, website content
Internal Company internal use only, limited to employees and authorized contractors Employee access only, no external sharing Internal procedures, employee directories, training materials
Confidential Sensitive business information, restricted to authorized personnel Encryption, access controls, need-to-know distribution Financial data, customer lists, strategic plans, contracts
Restricted Highly sensitive information, need-to-know basis only Strict access controls, audit trails, limited distribution Mergers and acquisitions, legal matters, security incidents
5. BOF-Specific Classification Examples

The following examples illustrate how BOF information should be classified according to business context and sensitivity.

  • Public Company website, marketing brochures, public safety information, press releases
  • Internal Employee handbooks, internal policies, training materials, operational procedures
  • Confidential Customer data, financial reports, driver information, dispatch schedules, pricing
  • Restricted Security incident details, merger negotiations, legal strategy, executive compensation
6. Labeling and Marking Requirements

All classified information must be properly labeled to ensure appropriate handling and protection.

  • Document Labeling All documents must include classification header and footer with level and handling instructions
  • Electronic Marking Email subjects, file names, and metadata indicate classification level
  • Physical Marking Confidential and restricted documents marked with appropriate labels and watermarks
  • System Classification Electronic systems enforce classification through access controls and metadata
7. Access Control and Authorization

Access to classified information is strictly controlled based on job function, need-to-know, and clearance level.

  • Authorization Access granted based on job function, classification level, and business need
  • Review Process Quarterly access reviews and immediate revocation when no longer required
  • Audit Trail Comprehensive logging of all access to classified information
  • Exception Process Formal approval process for temporary access exceptions
8. Data Handling and Storage

Classified information must be handled and stored according to specific security requirements based on classification level.

Storage Requirements:

  • Public: Standard storage with basic security controls
  • Internal: Company network storage with access controls
  • Confidential: Encrypted storage with role-based access controls
  • Restricted: Highly secure storage with multi-factor authentication and audit logging
9. Transmission and Sharing

Transmission of classified information must follow specific security protocols based on classification level.

Transmission Requirements:

  • Public: Standard email and web transmission
  • Internal: Company email and secure internal networks
  • Confidential: Encrypted transmission and secure file transfer protocols
  • Restricted: Encrypted transmission with additional authentication and logging
10. Classification Review and Updates

Information classification must be reviewed periodically and updated when sensitivity or business context changes.

Review Triggers:

  • Annual classification review by data owners
  • Changes in business context or regulatory requirements
  • Security incidents or data breaches
  • Organizational changes or restructuring
  • New technology implementations
11. Roles and Responsibilities

Specific roles and responsibilities are defined for effective information classification management.

Key Responsibilities:

  • Data Owners: Classify information and approve access requests
  • Data Custodians: Implement security controls and manage storage
  • Users: Handle information according to classification requirements
  • Security Team: Monitor compliance and enforce policies
  • Auditors: Review classification effectiveness and compliance
12. Policy Violations and Enforcement

Violations of this policy may result in disciplinary action, up to and including termination of employment, and may be reported to appropriate regulatory authorities.

Violation Examples:

  • Unauthorized access to classified information
  • Failure to properly label classified documents
  • Sharing classified information with unauthorized parties
  • Improper storage or transmission of classified data
  • Failure to report classification breaches or violations
