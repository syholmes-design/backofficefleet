This SOP does not replace BOF equipment availability, dispatch-release, or Secure Pickup authorization. A completed paper form is not a dispatch release. Structured BOF inspection / DVIR records remain the system of record when they exist for the trip.

1. Purpose

Define how drivers inspect commercial motor vehicles before and after dispatch, how defects are reported, and how equipment is held until a competent person clears the defect. This procedure supports BOF-SAF-POL-001 Section 5.

2. Authority labels

Regulatory requirement 49 CFR 392.7 addresses equipment inspection and use. 49 CFR 396.11 addresses driver vehicle inspection reports. 49 CFR 396.13 addresses driver inspection. Confirm current text with counsel or the official eCFR before treating any local practice as a complete legal program.

Company policy Drivers operating for this employer complete a pre-trip inspection before the first loaded or dispatched movement of the day on that unit, and a post-trip inspection at the end of the duty assignment, unless the BOF structured workflow for that trip already captured the same inspection.

Best practice Photograph defects when safe to do so and attach them to the BOF equipment or trip record.

Legal / compliance review required This SOP does not list a complete regulatory defect catalog, out-of-service criteria, or inspection-item schedule. North American Standard Out-of-Service Criteria and any customer-specific inspection rules require separate review.

3. Who does what

RoleResponsibility
DriverInspect the assigned power unit and, when used, the coupled trailer. Report defects before moving the vehicle if the defect affects safe operation. Certify the inspection in BOF or on the compatible paper form.
DispatcherDo not treat a unit as dispatch-ready when BOF shows an open safety defect or the driver reports an unresolved defect. Dispatch-release authority remains the existing BOF operating rule, not this document.
Maintenance / competent personReview reported defects, repair or defer according to company maintenance practice, and record clearance. Do not invent a second maintenance system of record if BOF already stores the defect.
SafetyReview repeated defects, OOS events, and roadside inspection results. Escalate under BOF-SAF-SOP-002 when a claim or incident is involved.

4. Procedure — pre-trip

  1. Confirm unit number, trailer number (if any), and that the assignment in BOF matches the physical equipment.
  2. Walk the vehicle. Check at least: tires and wheels, brakes, lighting and reflectors, steering, coupling, cargo securement (if loaded), emergency equipment, and obvious leaks or damage.
  3. If BOF already has a structured pre-trip / DVIR for this assignment, complete that workflow. Do not create a conflicting second inspection.
  4. If the structured workflow is unavailable, complete BOF-SAF-FORM-001 Section B or the paper counterpart and file it to the trip / equipment record.
  5. If a defect affects safe operation, stop. Notify dispatch. Do not begin the trip until a competent person clears the defect or the unit is replaced.

5. Procedure — post-trip and defect report

  1. At the end of the assignment, inspect the vehicle again and report defects discovered in transit or at destination.
  2. Enter the defect in the BOF equipment / DVIR record when that record exists. Paper BOF-SAF-FORM-001 may be used as a field capture aid.
  3. Identify whether the unit may remain in service, must be held, or requires roadside assistance.
  4. The next driver must review prior defect reports before operating the same unit. 49 CFR 396.13

6. Hold and clearance

Company policy An open safety defect holds the unit in BOF equipment availability until maintenance records clearance. This document does not change production equipment-authority logic; it describes the expected operating behavior.

Clearance requires: defect description, repair or deferral note, name of the person who cleared the unit, and date-time. Attach work-order or invoice references when they exist.

7. Roadside inspection and out-of-service

If a roadside inspection places a unit or driver out of service, the driver notifies dispatch immediately, retains the inspection report, and does not operate until the OOS condition is corrected. Record the event in the safety / equipment file. Citation details and fines are operational records, not this SOP’s authority.

Legal / compliance review required Federal and state out-of-service follow-up, including any 396.9 movement restrictions, must be confirmed for the jurisdiction of the stop.
