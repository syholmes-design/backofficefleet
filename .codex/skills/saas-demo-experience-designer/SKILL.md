# SaaS Demo Experience Designer

Use this skill when work involves the buyer-facing story or whether the website sells BOF quickly.

## Purpose

Make the website/demo explain BOF fast enough for a trucking company owner to understand the value without a long verbal explanation, while keeping the demo as robust and detail-rich as the original BOF experience.

The audience is a buyer or evaluator, not an internal project team. Demo robustness must be expressed as BOF operating value, not as notes about static pages, route strategy, implementation constraints, or how the demo was constructed.

## When To Use

- Homepage story
- Demo journey
- Before/after flow
- Founding fleet pitch
- Call-to-action placement
- Buyer confusion
- 60-second product comprehension
- Demo robustness and depth
- Command-centered story framing
- Operational proof points
- Persona-specific walkthroughs
- Client-ready demo standards

## Reference Standard

Use `bof-web-Original` as the attention-to-detail benchmark. Do not copy its route maze, but preserve the seriousness that came from:

- Persona demo paths.
- Guided operations walkthroughs.
- Route-backed surfaces.
- Data, documents, proof, statuses, consequences, owners, and next actions.
- Clear boundaries between public, guided, trusted, and internal demo surfaces.

The new `Website` should be simpler than the old app, but not less credible.

## Procedure

1. Identify the target buyer and the page's sales job.
2. State the product promise in plain language.
3. Keep the story tied to a credible command-centered operating path rather than a generic feature tour.
4. Shape a guided demo journey, not a full app tour.
5. Add enough operational proof for the page to feel real: statuses, owners, documents, route/load/driver context, consequence, what is blocked or cleared, and next action.
6. Preserve role-based understanding even when the visible site is simpler than the reference app.
7. Make CTAs obvious, credible, and non-competing.
8. Remove generic SaaS phrasing and replace it with transportation-specific value.
9. Flag any page that is visually polished but too thin to stand beside the original demo.
10. Flag any page that is visually clean but copy-sparse. A simple BOF demo still needs enough scenario language, proof, ownership, statuses, and consequence to feel serious.
11. Flag any visible copy that sounds like a developer note, internal demo note, QA note, or implementation explanation.
12. Keep Founding Fleet as a dedicated early-offer funnel. Do not make it the global homepage story, universal demo CTA, interactive-demo entry CTA, or repeated site-wide motif unless the user explicitly reopens that direction.

## Checks

- Can a trucking company owner understand BOF in 60 seconds?
- Does the homepage lead naturally into the demo?
- Is there a clear before/after story?
- Is the founding fleet offer clear?
- Is the founding fleet offer bounded to its own funnel instead of competing with the main BOF operating-layer story?
- Is the next step obvious?
- Does each primary demo page feel robust enough for a serious client review?
- Does each page have enough written substance to explain what is happening without a presenter rescuing it?
- Is simplification preserving proof instead of removing substance?
- Does the demo show consequences and ownership, not only cards and claims?
- Does the story feel anchored by a real operating system, not just page polish?
- Does every visible line sound appropriate for a trucking company owner or operations leader?
- Has implementation language such as "static demo," "proof file," "HTML text," "route maze," "reference demo," or "internal workspace" been removed from buyer-facing pages?

## Safety Boundaries

- Do not invent claims, testimonials, integrations, or pricing commitments.
- Do not expand into backend/product architecture.
- Keep `bof-web-Original` reference-only.
- Do not confuse a shorter demo with a shallow demo.
- Do not remove operational detail unless a clearer, customer-safe proof replaces it.
- Do not accept beautiful screens that are disconnected from the command-centered BOF operating story.
- Do not let internal build language leak into public-facing website copy.
- Do not reintroduce Founding Fleet everywhere while trying to strengthen CTAs. General pages should default to `Try Demo`, `Book Demo`, `BOF working session`, or an operations-record path.
