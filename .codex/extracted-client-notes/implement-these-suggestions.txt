Based on everything you've described about BOF, I would say yes, imagery is one of the areas that still has room for improvement, but it is probably not the biggest factor preventing a conversation with Alvys or a fleet prospect.

What BOF does.
Why it is different.
Why a fleet owner should care.
Imagery supports those goals, but does not replace them.

What I think is missing
If I were evaluating BOF as a prospective customer, I would be more interested in seeing:
Actual driver packets
Actual DQF reviews
Actual settlement packets
Actual compliance exceptions
Actual load workflows
than additional truck photographs.
Realistic artifacts create credibility.
One area I would invest in
A consistent illustration style.
Earlier you mentioned wanting small cartoon or illustrated elements. I think that is a good idea if done carefully.
Something similar to:
Modern SaaS illustrations
Logistics operations sketches
Clean flat-vector artwork
Light industrial/compliance themes
Avoid:
Comic-book style
Heavy caricatures
Mascots
Cartoon trucks everywhere
Fleet owners spending $10,000–$20,000 per month expect a professional operations platform, not a playful consumer product.
Priority ranking
If I were allocating effort today:
Improve workflow realism.
Improve document realism.
Improve dashboards and exception handling.
Improve screenshots and UI consistency.
Improve imagery.
So yes, the imagery can be improved, but I would not delay outreach solely because of the imagery. A fleet prospect will forgive average photography much faster than they will forgive a workflow that does not feel authentic.
