Aggregator problem

Many fleet relationships do not automatically become usable capacity.

Aggregator partners often know many fleets, but the operational question is narrower: which fleets are ready, compliant, document-complete, and usable for a specific customer or network opportunity?

Availability gap

Available capacity is not the same as usable capacity.

A carrier may be in the network while its driver files, insurance, equipment records, proof requirements, or customer approvals still need review.

Reputation risk

Referring an unready carrier can damage trust.

When a fleet is not ready, the aggregator can absorb the relationship damage even if the issue was documentation, proof, or administrative follow-up.

Scale pressure

Manual follow-up is hard to scale.

Compliance chasing, document collection, proof packet organization, and exception follow-up become difficult when the network grows.

Role boundary

Aggregators may want network value without operating every detail.

BOF gives partners a way to support network readiness without turning the partner into a day-to-day dispatcher or compliance manager.

Operating clarity

The network needs status before action.

Ready, needs review, blocked, and eligible-with-conditions records should be visible before a customer-facing workflow depends on them.

Proof burden

Proof rules have to follow the record.

Customer proof, POD, seal, lumper, claims, and settlement documents need to stay attached to the carrier, driver, customer, and equipment combination.

Commercial models

Four ways an aggregator partner can use BOF.

BOF's aggregator model is based on helping partners convert fleet relationships into a structured readiness network. The partner pays BOF for assessment, implementation, onboarding support, and ongoing readiness visibility. Referred fleets may later become BOF customers, but the core model is not a referral-fee or revenue-share program.
